            How to Play

            The Goal

            Every puzzle hides a code made of digits (3, 4, or 5 digits, depending on difficulty). A set of clues is given. Exactly one code fits every clue. Work it out and enter it. Only the Agent difficulty can repeat a digit within a code; in every other difficulty all the digits are different. A guess that repeats a digit in those difficulties is not counted and costs nothing.

            Reading the Clues

            Feedback clue
            Shows a guess and how it scored, with pips and in words: how many of its digits are in the code and in the right place, and how many are in the code but in a different place. A digit that is not in the code scores nothing.
            Sum clue
            The digits of the code add up to the number shown.
            Parity clue
            The digit in the stated position is even or odd. Zero is even.
            Compare clue
            The digit in one position is greater than, or less than, the digit in another position.
            Even-count clue
            The number shown is how many digits of the code are even. Zero counts as even.

            Positions count from the left, starting at 1: the first dial is the leftmost one.

            Rookie puzzles use feedback clues only. Agent adds parity and sum clues. Hacker and Master use every clue type.

            Worked Example

            A Rookie-style puzzle has 3 different digits and feedback clues only. This one has 4 clues.

            1. Guess 7 2 9: No digit is correct.
            2. Guess 6 8 2: 1 digit is correct and in the right place.
            3. Guess 1 5 9: 1 digit is correct and in the right place.
            4. Guess 4 2 1: 2 digits are correct but in the wrong place.

            The first clue rules out 7, 2 and 9. In the fourth clue the 2 is already out, so the two correct digits are 4 and 1, and neither is where the guess put it: 4 is not first and 1 is not third. The third clue has one correct digit, in the right place, and its 9 is out. The 1 is in the code, so the 1 is that digit, and it is in the right place: first. That also means 5 is not in the code. The second clue has one correct digit, in the right place, and its 2 is out. The 6 cannot be first, because the 1 is there, and nothing in this clue is in a different place, so the 6 is not in the code. That leaves the 8, and it is second. The code starts 1 8, and the missing digit is the 4, third. The answer is 184.

            Scoring

            Each solved puzzle earns the base points for its difficulty, multiplied by a speed bonus, an accuracy factor and a hint factor.

            • Speed bonus: starts at 2 and shrinks as time passes, but never falls below 0.5. The clock never stops while you play: switching tabs does not pause it.
            • Accuracy: each wrong guess lowers the factor by 8 percentage points, to a minimum of 40 percent.
            • Hint: using a hint cuts your points to 50 percent.

            Illustrative numbers only: with base points of 100, a solve at the target time (the speed bonus is then 1.25) with one wrong guess (accuracy 0.92) and no hint scores 100 × 1.25 × 0.92 = 115 points.

            Classic points, Blood points (from Blood mode) and total points are always shown separately.

            Blood Mode

            • A 3:00 countdown. Solve as many puzzles as you can before it reaches zero.
            • Every solve is worth ×2 points.
            • A wrong guess takes 5 seconds off the clock.
            • Skipping a puzzle takes 10 seconds off the clock.
            • Hints are off.
            • Puzzles get harder as you solve more of them.
            • The puzzle in play when the clock hits zero is lost. Points for puzzles you already solved are kept.
